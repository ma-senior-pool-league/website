title: Rules of play
order: 2
---
**Rule #1:** Out of courtesy and to avoid arguments, you *should* clearly call all your shots.
(Before a player shoots, his opponent or the referee should remind him to call the ball and
pocket). Even "obvious" shots to experienced players may not be obvious to rookies. Just call the
ball and the pocket. Details are immaterial. Also, you *must* call all non-obvious shots (e.g.
combinations, banks, kiss/billiard shots, or when there is more than one pocket you could be
shooting at). Again, just call the ball and the pocket. If you don't call a non-obvious shot and
the ball goes in, you lose your turn (just as you would, if you called a pocket and sank a ball in
another pocket). All balls pocketed in the game remain pocketed, except the cue ball.

**Rule #2:** To execute a legal break, the breaker (with the cue ball behind the head string) must
either pocket a ball or drive at least three numbered balls off a rail. If not, the opponent has
three options:

1. play the table as it is
2. give the shooter another chance to break
3. take the break, himself

When breaking, it is not necessary to hit the head ball (the ball that is on the head spot).

**Rule #3:** Scratching on the break gives the opponent the cue ball behind the head string. Any
other scratch or foul gives the opponent the cue ball anywhere on the table. This is known as
"ball in hand." When shooting from behind the head string after a break scratch, you may shoot
*directly* only at an object ball that is *in front of* or *on* the head string. Directly hitting
an object ball that is behind the head string is a foul.

**Rule #4:** If you sink the 8-ball on the break, *you win*, unless you scratch on the shot as
well, in which case you lose the game.

**Rule #5:** The table is always open immediately after the break shot, and it remains open until
a player sinks a legal called shot (i.e., without scratching or fouling). When the table is open,
it is legal to hit *any* solid or stripe ball first in the process of pocketing the called stripe
or solid. Hitting the 8-ball first is not allowed and is a foul.

**Rule #6:** A foul is committed (1) when you don't hit any of your object balls first; or (2)
*after* you hit one of your object balls first, no ball is pocketed or hits a rail. So, if *after*
you have hit one of your object balls first, *any* ball hits a rail or *any* ball (other than the
8-ball or cue ball) is pocketed, then there is no foul. If a player is about to shoot at the wrong
ball, then his opponent or referee should remind him what group of balls he should be shooting
for.

**Rule #7:** All of the shooter's balls are classified as object balls.

**Rule #8:** If you sink the 8-ball before all your balls, you lose; or, when shooting on the
8-ball, you sink it in a pocket you didn't call, you lose; or, if you foul in the process of
sinking the 8-ball, you lose; or, if you sink the 8-ball on the same stroke as the last of your
group of balls, you lose.

**Rule #9:** If any object ball is jumped off the table, it is a miss and loss of turn, not a foul.
If it is the 8-ball, it is loss of game. The shooter's object balls are spotted, and any of the
opponent's jumped balls will be pocketed. A cue ball jumped off the table is treated the same as a
scratch.

**Rule #10:** If the shooting player intends to play safe by pocketing an object ball, then, prior
to the shot, he must declare a safety to the opponent. If this is not done and the shooter's ball
is pocketed, he will be required to shoot again.

**Rule #11:** If you scratch or foul when shooting on the 8-ball, you don't lose unless you sink
the 8.

**Rule #12:** If a player intentionally marks the table in any way before executing his shot and
does not remove the mark prior to the shot, he has committed a foul that gives his opponent "ball
in hand" anywhere on the table.

**Rule #13:** A double hit is a foul. That is, if the cue stick contacts the cue ball more than
once on a shot, the shot is a foul.

**Rule #14: *Accidental hit:*** If you inadvertently move balls (other than the cue ball, which
would be a foul) around with the bridge, cue butt, arm, etc., before, during, or after your shot,
your opponent has the option of leaving the balls where they are, or putting them where they were
or would be; and there is no foul. Exceptions: If the 8-ball is pocketed this way, it goes back
where it was; or, if the cue tip moves the cue ball without making a legitimate hit, it is a foul.

**Rule #15: *Split hit:*** If the cue ball strikes a legal object ball and a non-legal-ball or bank
at approximately the same instant, and it cannot be determined which was hit first, it will be
assumed that the legal ball was struck first, (Benefit of the doubt goes to the shooter).

**Rule #16: *Foot on Floor:*** It's a foul if a player shoots when, at least, one foot isn't in
contact with the floor. Foot attire must be normal in regard to size, shape, and the manner in
which it is worn.

**Rule #17: *Stalemate:*** If, after three consecutive safe shots by both players, it is clear that
no progress is being made, they may declare a stalemate and re-rack. If the two players cannot
agree, then (as is the case with other disagreements) the referee casts the deciding vote.

**Rule #18: *Object Ball Frozen to Cushion or Cue Ball:*** This rule applies to any shot where the
cue ball's first contact with a ball is with one that is frozen to a cushion or to the cue ball,
itself. After the cue ball makes contact with the frozen object ball, the shot must result in
either:

1. a ball being pocketed, or
2. the cue ball contacting a cushion, or
3. the frozen ball being caused to contact a bank attached to a separate rail, or
4. another object ball being caused to contact a cushion with which it was not already in contact.

Failure to satisfy one of these four requirements is a foul. A ball that is touching a cushion at
the start of a shot and then is forced into a cushion that is attached to the same rail is not
considered to have been driven to that cushion unless it leaves the cushion, contacts another
ball, and then contacts the cushion again. An object ball is not considered frozen to a cushion
unless it is examined and announced as such by either the referee or one of the players prior to
that object ball's being involved in a shot.

**Rule #19: *Player's Responsibility:*** It is the player's responsibility to be aware of all
rules, regulations, and schedules applying to competition. While tournament officials will make
every reasonable effort to have such information readily available to all players as appropriate,
the ultimate responsibility rests with the player.

**Rule #20: *Reminders:***

1. If you accidentally move one or more balls before, during, or after your shot, give your
   opponent the opportunity to leave or put back the balls
2. If you want to measure something, the only object you can use is your cue stick
3. While masse shots are OK, jump shots are not
4. Even when your opponent commits a foul, you do not have to accept it
